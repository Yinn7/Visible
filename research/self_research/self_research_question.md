# Self Research

## 1. Research Overview

### Research Objective

Understand my own experience with nighttime itching and scratching, including how I notice it, how it affects my sleep and daily life, how I currently manage it, and what information could help me better understand my nighttime behavior.

This self-study will also explore whether a wearable monitoring device could meaningfully support this experience, and what requirements such a device would need to meet.

### Research Questions

1. How does nighttime itching and scratching affect my sleep and daily experience?
2. How aware am I of scratching that happens during sleep?
3. How do I currently know whether I scratched during the night?
4. What do I currently do to manage nighttime itching or scratching?
5. What information would be useful to me the next morning?
6. Would a nighttime monitoring device provide useful information or support?
7. What would make such a device acceptable or unacceptable to use during sleep?
8. What design requirements can be derived from my experience?

---

# 2. Research Method

### Study Type

Self-observation / Self-study

### Duration

7 days

### Participant

Researcher / Potential User

### Data Sources

- Pre-sleep self-report
- Morning self-report
- Daily reflection
- Current skin condition
- Current nighttime management strategies
- Future wearable sensor data, if applicable

### Study Approach

The first part of the study focuses on observing my existing nighttime experience without introducing a new device or changing my normal routine.

The second part explores my expectations and concerns regarding a potential nighttime monitoring device.

### Important Limitation

This study represents one individual's experience and cannot be treated as representative of all potential users.

The findings should be compared with additional user research before making broader design conclusions.

# 9. Limitations

- This study involved only one participant.
- The participant is also the researcher, which may introduce confirmation and interpretation bias.
- Self-reported nighttime behavior may be incomplete or inaccurate.
- I may not be aware of unconscious scratching during sleep.
- Morning observations may not accurately indicate when or how scratching occurred.
- Wearable sensor data, if collected later, should not automatically be treated as ground truth.
- Findings should be compared with additional potential users before making broader claims.
