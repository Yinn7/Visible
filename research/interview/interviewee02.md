## Key Observations

1. **Nighttime scratching can happen without awareness.**
   The participant described scratching while asleep and sometimes waking with their hand already scratching.

2. **Severe scratching can directly disrupt sleep.**
   The participant may be awakened by scratching, with bleeding and visible blood on clothing or bedding.

3. **Current awareness is largely retrospective.**
   Blood stains, wounds, and skin condition are used to infer what happened during sleep.

4. **The participant wants highly detailed nighttime information.**
   They expressed strong interest in knowing when scratching started, which body part was scratched, and what happened around the event.

5. **Nighttime information could support preventive action.**
   If the participant knew a particular area was likely to itch at night, they would apply medication or take other preventive steps before sleeping.

6. **Doctors do not usually explore nighttime experiences in depth.**
   The participant felt that limited consultation time makes detailed nighttime discussion difficult and that patients often need to bring up sleep problems themselves.

7. **The participant already uses personal routines to manage symptoms.**
   Medication, topical treatments, moisturizing, environmental control, and monitoring stress are integrated into their self-care.

8. **Wearable adoption appears relatively high when the device is unobtrusive.**
   The participant already sleeps with a smartwatch and values detailed health tracking.

9. **Lightweight and long battery life are important practical requirements.**
   The participant preferred a device that is small, simple, easy to wear, and does not require frequent charging.

10. **The psychosocial impact was stronger during childhood.**
    The participant described significant self-consciousness and social pressure when younger, while the impact has decreased with age and greater acceptance.

---

## Initial Design Implications

- Morning reports should show **when and where suspected scratching occurred**.
- Consider showing **event context or sequence**, not only aggregate numbers.
- Data could support **preventive nighttime care**, rather than only retrospective reflection.
- The system should not assume that users will discuss nighttime symptoms with doctors unless prompted.
- Keep the wearable **small, lightweight, comfortable, and low-maintenance**.
- Consider allowing users to connect nighttime data with their existing care routines.
- Psychosocial context is important, but should not automatically become a product feature.

## Open Questions

- Would users prefer a timeline, summary, or visual replay?
- What nighttime information would be useful to doctors?
- Does seeing nighttime data consistently lead to preventive action?
- How accurate does the system need to be for users to trust the information?
