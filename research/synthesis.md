# Research Synthesis

## Research Sources

This synthesis is based on three research sources:

- Interview 01
- Interview 02
- Researcher Self-Observation (7 days)

The findings below focus on nighttime scratching, awareness and recall, existing self-care practices, information needs, and expectations for a nighttime wearable device.

---

# Research Findings

## 1. Nighttime Scratching Can Happen With Limited Awareness

Nighttime scratching occurred across the research sources. Scratching was not always fully conscious or remembered. In some cases, more severe scratching could wake a person during the night.

Physical evidence after waking, such as irritated or damaged skin, could also help reveal that scratching had occurred even when the event itself was not clearly remembered.

---

## 2. Nighttime Scratching Is Difficult to Fully Recall

People may know that they scratched during the night but have difficulty reconstructing exactly what happened.

Details that can be difficult to recall include:

- When scratching happened
- How long it lasted
- How often it occurred
- Which body areas were affected

This creates an information gap between experiencing nighttime scratching and understanding the specific scratching events that occurred during sleep.

---

## 3. Self-Care Is Already Part of Participants' Routines

Skin care and symptom management were already part of participants' daily or nighttime routines.

Reported practices included:

- Moisturizing
- Medication
- Cooling
- Environmental adjustments

Participants were therefore already taking active steps to manage their skin condition rather than relying on monitoring alone.

---

## 4. Users Want Specific Nighttime Scratching Information

The most consistent information needs across the research centered on specific characteristics of nighttime scratching:

- **Duration** — how long scratching lasted
- **Location** — which body areas were affected
- **Frequency** — how often scratching occurred
- **Timing** — when scratching happened during the night

These types of information address details that can be difficult to perceive or remember while sleeping.

The researcher self-observation additionally identified potential value in comparing scratching patterns over time, but this was not treated as a consistent need across the interviews.

---

## 5. Nighttime Information Could Inform Self-Care

Participants described ways that more specific nighttime scratching information could support their existing self-care.

For example, knowing which areas were repeatedly scratched could help them pay more attention to those areas during skin care. Nighttime information could therefore provide additional context for decisions participants were already making about how to manage their skin.

---

## 6. A Wearable Should Minimize Sleep Interference

Participants emphasized that a nighttime monitoring device should not become another source of discomfort or sleep disruption.

Important device expectations included:

- Lightweight
- Comfortable
- Easy to wear
- Hard to notice during sleep
- Accurate

The wearable should be able to collect useful scratching information while interfering as little as possible with normal sleep.

---

## 7. Participants Were Generally Open to Nighttime Monitoring

Participants were generally open to using a wearable device to collect nighttime scratching data.

This suggests that wearable monitoring may be acceptable in this context when the device is comfortable, unobtrusive, and able to provide useful information.

---

# Core Finding

> People experience nighttime scratching but may not fully perceive or recall what happens during sleep. They want accurate and specific information about when, where, how often, and how long they scratch so they can better understand these nighttime events and support their existing self-care, as long as the monitoring device does not interfere with sleep.

---

# Key User Needs

Based on the findings, the primary user needs are:

1. **Awareness**  
   Users need a way to understand scratching events that happen while they are asleep or only partially aware.

2. **Specific Information**  
   Users need information about scratching timing, frequency, duration, and location.

3. **Connection to Self-Care**  
   Users need nighttime information that can be interpreted in relation to their existing skin-care practices.

4. **Low Sleep Interference**  
   Users need monitoring that does not noticeably interfere with sleep.

5. **Reliable Data**  
   Users need the device to collect scratching information accurately enough to be useful.

---

# Design Implications

The research suggests several directions for the prototype:

- Prioritize detection of nighttime scratching events.
- Focus initial data output on timing, frequency, duration, and location where technically feasible.
- Present nighttime data in a form that users can review after waking.
- Support existing self-care practices rather than requiring a completely new care routine.
- Keep the wearable lightweight, comfortable, and unobtrusive during sleep.
- Prioritize sensing accuracy because users expect the recorded information to reliably represent their nighttime scratching.

These implications should guide the MVP but should not be treated as direct research findings themselves.
