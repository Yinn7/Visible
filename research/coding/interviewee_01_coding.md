## Key Observations

1. **Nighttime scratching may happen outside conscious awareness.**
   The participant described scratching while partially asleep and sometimes waking because of it.

2. **Nighttime behavior is mainly reconstructed in the morning.**
   The participant relies on skin condition and physical evidence rather than direct nighttime monitoring.

3. **The participant wants event-level information.**
   The main interests are when scratching happened, how long it lasted, and where it occurred.

4. **Awareness may be a potential benefit of monitoring.**
   However, it is not yet clear whether seeing the data would actually change behavior.

5. **Wearability is a major constraint.**
   Comfort, movement, and awareness of the device during sleep affect willingness to use a wearable.

6. **Environmental and psychological factors provide context but are not yet product requirements.**
   These include stress, moisture, dust, food, and other perceived triggers.

---

## Initial Design Implications

- Prioritize **when / how long / where** rather than general sleep scores.
- Use **“suspected scratching”** rather than presenting detection as confirmed.
- Make the **morning report** the primary interaction.
- Minimize device awareness and sleep disruption.
- Treat environmental and psychological factors as **research context**, not automatic sensing requirements.
