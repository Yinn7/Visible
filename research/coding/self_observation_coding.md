## Key Observations

1. **Nighttime scratching is not always remembered.**  
   Some scratching was only inferred from physical evidence or remembered after waking.

2. **Awareness varies across nights.**  
   The researcher sometimes woke while scratching but was completely unaware on other nights.

3. **Sleep impact varies.**  
   Scratching did not always disrupt sleep, but severe episodes could cause awakening.

4. **Morning information is limited.**  
   Physical evidence can suggest that scratching happened, but does not show its timing, frequency, duration, or location.

5. **The most desired information is event-level information.**  
   The researcher consistently wanted to know when, how often, how long, and where scratching occurred.

6. **Nighttime data could support self-care.**  
   Knowing nighttime patterns could influence moisturizing, medication, and environmental adjustments.

7. **Device comfort is critical.**  
   The device should be lightweight, unobtrusive, comfortable, and should not interfere with sleep or irritate the skin.

---

## Emerging Insight

> There is a gap between nighttime scratching and morning awareness: the researcher may know that scratching probably happened, but cannot reconstruct what happened during the night.

This supports exploring whether a monitoring device can provide useful nighttime information without disrupting sleep.
